An expert's caller and the creator it represents can be different people. Maintaining a published expert therefore requires deciding whose observations should change which rules. We introduce CogniLife, a framework for continuous cognitive distillation in multi-expert agent marketplaces. The framework addresses the cold-expert problem: a listing can receive many requests while its creator's relevant activity remains elsewhere. Seeding, Immersion, and Crystallization organize creator-authored commitments, attributed observations, and revisable guidance. Creator-authorized authoring, owner-scoped memory maintenance, skill proposals, and publication provide distinct control points; Creator Profiles and Expert Journals define the proposed cross-expert extension. Deployment evaluation on advising, frozen-skill execution, and customs-extraction tasks yields six checkable lessons for expert marketplaces: match base models in configuration comparisons, keep scoring materials out of generation, bind scores to output hashes, budget judge outputs uniformly, log tool failures, and validate gate-admitted skills by execution before promotion. A 52-task customs-extraction case study adds three recorded valid runs per task, a mean recorded score of 0.958, mean runtime of 9.71 minutes, and mean estimated cost of USD 1.02 per run. The framework makes evidence ownership, applicability, and update authority explicit in the maintenance of creator-representing experts.
